Backup

When your host backs itself up. Each backup is encrypted on your host before it leaves, to your recovery key and your bastions' keys; no key that opens it is kept anywhere but with you. A host on its own keeps its backups in a folder on this machine, and a host on the optional Privatae network sends them to the network's storage, unless you choose another place below. Times are in your own time zone.

Your keys

Every backup is encrypted to your Host recovery key, and to the key of any bastion you add, and to nothing else. Your recovery key is made in your browser, in the Backups app on this host or, if you use the optional Privatae network, on your Privatae account page, and you keep it: nobody else has it. Without it your host takes no backups, because there would be nobody to encrypt them to.

Restore

Put your host back the way it was at one of its backups: its memory and data, and its apps and files. Your Git repositories stay as they are. Your host cannot open its backups by itself: first you give this page your recovery key, and your browser opens that backup's key file and seals it to this host (your recovery key never leaves your browser), or one of your bastions does it (you approve it on the bastion). Your host backs up how it is now first, so a restore can be undone the same way. Backups a lost host kept in platform storage show here only on a host with the same Host number; copies on your bastions show on any host of yours they are connected to.

Exports waiting for you

You asked for an export on your Privatae account page. Your host builds it only when you confirm it here, and only if it matches the fingerprint written in your export key file: an export is a full copy of your host, sealed to that key. The account page is served by Privatae, so check the fingerprint against your key file, not against the page.

Where backups go

Wherever they go, your backups leave your host already encrypted to your keys, so the place that keeps them cannot open them.

Use a key already in the Keys app

A key saved as JSON like {"access_key_id": "…", "secret_access_key": "…"}.

Your host keeps the key in its vault, sealed to it, and reads it only to send a backup. It is never copied into this setting, which would put it in every backup.

The machine pulls the copy down itself, over its own internet connection — nothing large passes through your host. It lands in the backup folder of that machine's bastion, with its key file beside it; point that folder at a drive or a network share it has mounted (a NAS, for instance). One thing to do first: in the Network app, approve a management grant for this machine covering the operation named backup_fetch. Without it the machine will refuse the transfer, which is the point of the grant.

The newest ones stay and older ones are removed. They are on the same machine as your host, so they do not help if the machine itself is lost: download one now and then and keep it somewhere else. The Backups app restores a downloaded file, here or on another C-Series machine.
