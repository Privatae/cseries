If you have built a Host for someone else, such as a client, you can hand it over. You release the Host to their email address and get a one-time code; they accept it with that code from their own account. The Host itself is not moved or copied. Its disk, its database and everything installed on it stay exactly where they are, and what changes is who it belongs to.

## How do I hand a Host over?

In your account on privatae.ai, choose to transfer the Host and enter the other person's email address. You get a one-time code and a claim link, shown to you once, because only a fingerprint of the code is kept. The platform also emails them an invitation; if that email cannot be sent, the page tells you, and you pass the link and code on yourself.

Only the Host's owner can release it, a Host can have one open transfer at a time, and an offer that is not accepted lapses after a week. Nothing about the Host changes when you release it.

## What does the other person do?

They open the claim link, sign in with the email address you named, and enter the code. Both halves are needed: your release names them, and their acceptance proves it is them. If they do not have an account yet, one is created for them when they accept.

## Can I change my mind?

Yes. Until the other person accepts, you can withdraw the transfer from the same place you released it.

## What moves and what stays?

What stays is the Host: its disk, its database, its apps and projects, and what it remembers. The person receiving it gets the Host as it is, so prepare it the way you want them to have it. The Host keeps everything it holds, so before you release it, unpair your own machines, disconnect your git account in the Gitea app, take back any repository access you gave the Host there, and remove your keys.

What moves is ownership:

- They become the Host's owner, and every other account that could open the Host, yours included, loses that access.
- The Host moves onto their capacity. It arrives with no memory, keeping its storage, and they give it memory from their own capacity to start it (see [Plans and billing](wiki:plans-and-billing)). The memory it was using goes back to yours. If the Host had a plan of its own instead, that plan ends and you are refunded for the unused time.
